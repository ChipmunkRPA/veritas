# Protect the speech assessment when stereo cancels in a video

Chipmunk Verify checks speech on a mono copy of the first audio stream.
Stereo sound can cancel during that mix. The video evidence already
showed the loss in the stereo comparison, but a remaining mono voice
could still produce a recording-wide leaning towards original speech.
An entirely cancelled mono track could also be labeled silent despite
measurable sound in both original channels.

New video analyses reuse the [standalone audio coverage rule](audio-downmix-coverage-2026-11-15.md).
It requires opposing channels and a strong loss of mono level in at
least one complete, clock-aligned one-second window in the first 30
seconds. The report explains that sound was unavailable to the voice
check and gives no soundtrack confidence. Direct voice responses do not
enter the whole-video estimate. Existing measured windows remain available
as inspection scores, and the visual checks still run. The rule neither
identifies the missing sound nor says that it was synthesized.

The browser evidence and Standard PDF mark the affected interval. The
PDF links the speech assessment to the stereo comparison. The JSON
points to the measured stereo windows at
`/audio/coverage/mono_voice_coverage_limit`. The waveform and left,
right and analyzed mono excerpts use the preserved soundtrack clock.
Old reports keep their frozen results. New analyses apply the rule.

## Local verification

Six three-second H.264 MP4s shared the same generated test pattern.
They carried stereo ALAC or AAC sound from the earlier held audio
fixtures. Two clips cancel some sound between 1 and 2 seconds. A third
carries audible opposing channels in that interval and silence
elsewhere. Its mono PCM matches the silent control exactly. The lossless
partial edit and its ordinary control also produce identical mono PCM.
The AAC deliveries have independent decoded mono hashes.

| Cases | Previous soundtrack result | With the coverage rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two partial cancellations | Both leaned towards original speech | Both inconclusive, without a speech confidence percentage |
| Complete cancellation in mono | Silent | Stereo cancellation explained, without a speech estimate |
| Three ordinary or silent controls | Two original-speech tendencies and one silent track | All three unchanged |

All 12 simulated $9.99 checkouts required confirmed payment before
creating an analysis. API and JSON evidence matched in every case.
All three affected reports displayed the same 1.000–2.000 s interval.
Signed packages verified the picture and soundtrack evidence against
the submitted videos. This comparison uses deliberate cancellation on
controlled clips. It does not measure how often cancellation occurs in
real recordings, or how well the detector finds speech masked by noise
inside either channel.

## Replay

The public numeric review needs only standard Python, the replay script
and the receipt. To reproduce native inference, first regenerate the
local WAVs from the [standalone audio replay](audio-downmix-coverage-2026-11-15.md#replay).
Use new working directories and the existing locally installed voice
and video assets. The picture is generated by FFmpeg. The source audio
and playback assets stay local.

```sh
export PYTHONPATH=backend OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=2 VECLIB_MAXIMUM_THREADS=2
module=scripts.video_downmix_coverage_app
python3 -B -S -m "$module" review docs/evaluation-panels/video-soundtrack-downmix-coverage-2026-11-15.json
backend/.venv/bin/python -B -m "$module" prepare "$standalone_audio_replay" "$run/fixtures"
backend/.venv/bin/python -B -m "$module" before "$run/fixtures" "$run/before"
backend/.venv/bin/python -B -m "$module" after "$run/fixtures" "$run/after"
```
