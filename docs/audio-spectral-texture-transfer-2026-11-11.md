# Spectral texture for speech under competing voices

This experiment tests whether a lightweight, mono-compatible measurement can identify unreliable AI flags under recorded competing speech. It compares one regularized head trained to keep its score stable across matched clean and noisy versions with the same head trained without that penalty. Each uses a separately fixed diagnostic operating point. [Exact aggregate](evaluation-panels/audio-spectral-texture-transfer-2026-11-11.json)

## Fixed inputs and methods

The fit uses 102 three-second views from 34 exact foregrounds. Three entire connected components of the earlier matched-babble experiment and earlier stereo controls supply the fit. A fourth held babble component and earlier coherence controls supply 54 point-selection views. Keep all acoustic variants of a foreground in one role. The fit and point-selection roles share no exact foreground or declared source group. The stereo and coherence additions use genuine recordings and designed Alania voices. The same-source penalty uses 68 paired changes. The previously tested source domains and synthetic methods limit the comparison. [Matched babble controls](audio-matched-babble-v181-result.md) · [Stereo controls](audio-stereo-component-transfer-2026-11-11.md) · [Coherence controls](audio-natural-stereo-coherence-2026-11-11.md)

Compute log-magnitude transforms on full Hann windows of 256 and 1,024 samples with a quarter-window hop. Remove the broadband median within each frame and the median over time at each frequency. In each of six bands from 120 Hz to 7.5 kHz, summarize the residual magnitude and its absolute time and frequency differences by their median and 80th percentile. The descriptor has 72 values. It has no neural encoder or imported checkpoint. A weighted logistic head gives each origin class the same total weight and each foreground within its class the same total weight. The paired arm adds a squared-logit penalty when a noisy view's logit departs from its matched clean view. The ordinary arm omits the paired term. Prior work motivates evaluating time and frequency structure under processing and separating changes in foreground from changes in the acoustic scene. [Time-frequency consistency research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17761v2) · [Independent speech and background manipulation study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17617) · [Source and generator diversity study](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.796/)

For each arm, select the highest point that preserves at least 90% of existing synthetic AI calls overall and at least 90% on clean point-selection clips. Freeze both heads and points before transfer inference. The only possible verdict change is withholding a current AI direction when the independent head falls below its point. A withheld flag becomes inconclusive. The same current speech gate and classifier calibration apply to every arm. Advancement requires at least two distinct genuine false flags removed, at least one per noisy scene, while losing at most one correct AI flag and assessing at least six genuine inputs in each scene.

The transfer contains 80 planned views. Twelve genuine RUEG Turkish readers and 14 Turkish synthetic utterances provide 26 distinct foreground recordings. The synthetic set has six designed Alania voices, four Okur utterances from one stock voice, and four Drifting utterances with older project exposure. Genuine and synthetic examples are all Turkish in the transfer. Known exact source groups remain disjoint from fitting and point selection. Each utterance contributes a clean control and two −5 dB mono SNR mixtures. The interfering speech comes from two exact new cuts from earlier AMI sessions, with no time overlap with the previous native selections. Both interfering speech clips are also assessed alone. ## Local transfer

Both fixed heads kept all 18 existing synthetic AI calls in the separate point-selection role. Each also kept all eight clean synthetic calls in that role. Neither withheld a genuine false flag there.

The current detector made no false AI calls on the 35 assessed genuine views. Twelve genuine clips were inconclusive in the first noisy scene, and 11 were inconclusive in the second. One clean genuine view was unassessed. Both isolated interfering-speech clips passed speech admission and remained inconclusive. All 42 synthetic views were assessed, and none leaned original. The paired veto lost three existing synthetic AI directions, including one clean clip. The ordinary veto lost only the clean clip. The advancement requirement failed for both heads.

On identical assessed inputs, the current score ranked genuine and synthetic speech better than either spectral head. Its ROC AUC was 0.964 and 1.000 in the noisy scenes. The paired head reached 0.768 and 0.280. The ordinary head reached 0.702 and 0.256. In this sample, nine and 14 synthetic raw scores exceeded every genuine raw score in their respective scenes. The fixed current rule called two and five of those synthetic clips AI. This observed score ordering suggests a calibration and coverage gap on these particular scenes. It supplies no new operating point for future uploads.

A separate retrospective replay examined all 53 stored inputs from the earlier English MUSAN study, which had five known genuine false flags. The fitted heads and points stayed frozen. In the first background, the paired head would have reduced genuine false flags from four to two and synthetic true flags from five to four. The ordinary head would have reduced the genuine false flags to three and the true flags to four. Both retained the one genuine false flag and all ten synthetic true flags in the second background. These exact archived WAVs were outside the fitting and point-selection roles. Their earlier outcomes were already known, and their backgrounds are different from the prospectively tested competing speech.
