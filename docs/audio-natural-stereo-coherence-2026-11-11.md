# Two-channel noise filtering for synthetic speech screening

At −5 dB input SNR, a two-channel filter recovered nine missed synthetic-speech flags across two recorded meeting backgrounds. It also added false flags on three genuine readers, all in the second scene. The filtered view remains experimental. [Exact aggregate](evaluation-panels/audio-natural-stereo-coherence-2026-11-11.json)

## Fixed inputs and rule

Take three-second excerpts from six genuine readers, three English LibriSpeech readers and three Turkish VoxForge contributors. The synthetic excerpts comprise four designed Alania voices, four Okur utterances from one stock voice, and four Drifting utterances from three stock voices. The 14 non-Drifting foregrounds were unused in the immediately preceding temporal score comparison. The four Drifting utterances were used in the older kitchen and traffic evaluation. Model pretraining overlap is unknown. [LibriSpeech release](https://www.openslr.org/12/) · [VoxForge rights review](audio-voxforge-tr-genuine-v192-method.md) · [Alania preparation](audio-alania-followup-v236-method.md) · [Okur preparation](audio-okur-turkish-followup-v245-method.md) · [Drifting preparation](audio-drifting-turkish-v215-method.md) · [Earlier kitchen and traffic trial](audio-fresh-shared-noise-score-v219-result.md)

Use two microphone tracks from the University of Edinburgh AMI corpus for each of two meeting sessions under CC BY 4.0. Test four untouched three-second windows per session on a new time grid. Use the window with the largest channel-difference-to-average RMS ratio in each session as the recorded interfering sound. The initial 0.01 normalized PCM RMS qualification left one session short. The frozen 0.002 level admits four windows per session. The level adjustment preceded all speech-gate and classifier calls. The earlier AMI trial used the same two sessions and different times. [AMI release license](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/corpus/license.shtml) · [Verified original microphone intake](audio-ami-native-stereo-v291-method.md) · [Earlier AMI decision trial](audio-ami-stereo-transfer-v293-result.md)

Make a matched clean two-channel copy and two mixtures per foreground. The foreground is equal in the two channels. The recorded interfering signal retains the original difference between microphones. Scale its mono RMS to −5 dB foreground-to-background SNR, with one clipping-safe gain across the three views for each source. The 54 controlled views and eight unchanged native views are locked together. Real recordings may have different foreground location, phase, and channel treatment.

The filter uses each mixture alone. Compute 512-sample periodic Hann transforms with a 128-sample hop. Smooth real cross-power and total channel power separately over seven frames with nearest-edge padding. Clip twice their ratio to [0, 1], and map it linearly to amplitude gains from 0.20 to 1 in 150–6,400 Hz. Leave other frequencies at unity and reconstruct the mean spectrum onto the exact original sample clock. Two-channel coherence motivates this frequency-domain weighting. The chosen parameters were fixed locally. [Primary dual-microphone coherence study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3246289/) · [Primary study on two-microphone noise reduction](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/24/12/3979)

Both the production mono and filtered view must pass the current speech gate before their own voice scores. Apply the existing classifier and calibration without fitting. The second opinion only changes a current inconclusive window to leaning AI when the filtered view leans AI. It preserves current original, AI and unassessed decisions. The predeclared requirement for another trial was at least two distinct synthetic voice groups recovered and zero new genuine false flags. The separate filtered-only arm shows how the processing changes admission and direction.

All controlled views passed speech admission on both arms. In the first scene the second opinion recovered one Alania, one Okur, and one Drifting result. In the second it recovered one Alania, one Okur, and four Drifting results. The nine added flags represent seven utterances in six declared voice groups. The three additional false flags in the second scene came from one English and two Turkish readers. Across both scenes the current rule falsely flagged two of the six distinct genuine readers at least once, and the second opinion falsely flagged five. All 18 clean filtered copies were byte-identical to the corresponding mono copies.

The measured SNR gain was 2.17 dB median in the first scene and 3.23 dB in the second. Both foreground and interference were replayed separately under the exact same filter mask for this measurement. The second scene yielded the largest acoustic gain and the most additional false flags. A clearer conditioned input therefore provides no basis on its own for stronger authorship confidence.

Of eight untouched native meeting windows, six passed the original speech gate. The original and second-opinion policies each returned one false AI direction, two original directions, three inconclusive and two unassessed. The filtered-only arm returned five inconclusive and three unassessed. Its withdrawal of the one false AI came with one fewer scored native view. These observations cover two meetings and six controlled genuine readers. Repeated use of the same foreground across both backgrounds is paired evidence.

The fixed candidate failed its advancement requirement. The current voice decision and overall confidence are unchanged. The run made 11,656 speech-gate frame forwards and 133 voice-classifier forwards, including stability checks, on 60 mono and 59 filtered inputs. A separate readout verified all 186 PCM commitments, the exact quantized background in 36 mixtures, eight source-clocked native windows, speech admissions, classifier calls, and paired decision arithmetic. Source recordings, transcripts, private identifiers and per-window score files stayed local.
