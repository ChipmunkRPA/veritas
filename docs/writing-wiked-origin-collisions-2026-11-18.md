# When an AI copyedit matches an archived revision

Eight of 18 offline editing responses reproduced the exact final sentence in an archived Wikipedia revision. Those replies came from two editor families across two articles. Placing the archived and generated wording in the same earlier article excerpt made each pair byte-identical. A check that sees only the resulting passage has the same input regardless of how the wording was obtained. [Grouped receipt](evaluation-panels/writing-wiked-origin-collisions-2026-11-18.json)

## How to reproduce the comparison

1. Use the first 512 KiB of the compressed English WikEd v1.0 release as a bounded index. The receipt records the prefix SHA-256. Before requesting text or scoring, rank candidate rows by a fixed hash of the corpus page and revision values. Keep distinct pages with copyediting or grammar comments, short marked edits and unchanged numbers. Exclude prior pilot examples, reversions and bot-like records. This produced a roster of 18 candidates from 173 eligible rows. [WikEd release](https://github.com/snukky/wikiedits#wiked-error-corpus) · [Official English archive](https://data.statmt.org/romang/wiked/wiked-v1.0.en.tgz)
2. Retrieve the revision and its parent using the publisher's revision history. Confirm that both belong to the same article in the main namespace. Match the marked sentence by its unique word sequence on each side and take the actual wording and character offsets from the rendered prose. The corpus page field and punctuation can differ from the publisher's record. Sixteen revision records resolved, four complete pairs were retrievable within the request limit, and three provided an earlier excerpt with 12–15 complete sentences. The indexed 2011–2014 revisions predate the tested models. The archive establishes when the text appeared. It cannot establish how a contributor wrote it. [MediaWiki Revisions API](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/API:Revisions) · [WikEd authors and corpus terms](https://www.junczys.net/publications/pdf/mjd.poltal2014.draft.pdf) · [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/)
3. Give two pinned Apache-2.0 instruction models only the earlier sentence. Ask for a small, meaning-preserving change to one interior phrase and run three attempts per sentence and model. The pilot used seed 96183, four-bit group-64 affine quantization, temperature 0.65, top-p 0.90 and a limit of 96 generated tokens. Collapse reply whitespace, check that both sides of a single replacement contain one to seven words, confirm numbers are unchanged, and require all characters outside it to match. Compare the complete result against the published revision before accepting an example for accuracy testing. [Pinned editor A](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct/tree/989aa7980e4cf806f80c7fef2b1adb7bc71aa306) · [Pinned editor B](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct/tree/31b70e2e869a7173562077fd711b654946d38674)
4. Replace the focal sentence in an earlier excerpt with each final reading. Check complete UTF-8 equality and preserve the same native sentence positions for the model and archive versions. Keep shared-output pairs as ambiguity controls. Use independently verified, meaning-preserving edits with different final wording for a localization accuracy trial.

## What the current passage check saw

All eight bounded replacements matched archived wording. Six came from editor A and two from editor B. Ten replies failed the shape or numeric checks. No distinct, meaning-preserving positive qualified for evaluation.

The collision replay used eight text views from the two affected articles. Each article contributed its earlier excerpt, the archived sentence placed in that excerpt, and one equal result from each editor. The archived sentence is attested in the later revision. Its surrounding excerpt in this test came from the earlier revision. The run used 66 distinct classifier inputs across isolated and contextual readings. Each of the four archive/editor comparisons reproduced the exact stored readings, sentence positions and selections. The pooled, within-document and report-confirmed selectors assessed all eight views and produced zero highlights. The app's check uses decoded passage text and reports sentence-range observations. [Passage selection and localization](writing-interval-localization-audit-2026-11-18.md)

This small pilot measures exact wording collisions and selector equality. It supplies neither a false-alarm rate nor a detection rate, and the response counts do not estimate collision prevalence. Each article is one independent source, even if several replies repeat the same edit. No product settings or confidence calculations changed. The public receipt contains grouped counts and an archive-level digest. Source passages, revision identifiers, contributor identities, prompts containing source text and generated wording remain local.
