# Three- and four-frame face changes in native 720p video

The current Full face review found no overlapping or useful range in 18
brief swap deliveries. Extending its existing boundary shortlist to
three- and four-frame pairs found both exact boundaries in 13 deliveries.
The extension had no alert on the 36 ordinary controls. Sixteen ordinary
controls were unassessed, leaving 20 assessed controls without an alert.

## Fixed comparison

Each of six source groups contributed a known three-frame swap, a known
four-frame swap, and a second encoding of the four-frame swap at CRF 35.
Ordinary controls included the untouched recording, a conventional encode,
a jump within the same take, a cut to a different real take, a brief
local exposure change, and a brief local blur. All deliveries used upright
720p AVC/H.264, 4:2:0, exactly 120 pictures on a native 30 fps clock. The
working panel used separately recorded target, donor, and component keys,
and excluded the source groups recorded for neighboring face studies.
Prior exposure elsewhere in the project or in pretrained weights remains
unknown. The test edits, matched controls, and encodes were authored for
this comparison. [Previous native AVC study](video-brief-face-avc-confirmation-2026-11-13.md)

## Local application verification

The optional development check uses the existing face appearance pass on
the tested native profile. The browser and report identify the half-open
frame interval and display original face crops from before, during, and
after it. The exact frame indices, timestamps, and review coverage are
retained in JSON and the signed package.

Six isolated analyses exercised the check in a simulated USD 9.99 checkout.
The three-frame source reproduced frames `[66, 69)` with witnesses 65,
67, and 69. The re-encoded four-frame source reproduced `[110, 114)`
with witnesses 109, 112, and 114. The remaining cases exercised a measured
no-range result, insufficient same-shot context, an unsupported source
clock, and the option disabled. All six signed packages replayed against
their original MP4 files. The overall decision was identical with the
option on and off for the same file. Each analysis started after simulated
payment confirmation.

## Transfer to native 29.97 fps

A separate comparison used six other native 29.97 fps recording groups
and newly authored exact swap boundaries. The output was encoded on either
a 30,000- or a 90,000-tick time base. The existing Full check did not find
a useful range in the 18 edited deliveries. The candidate found 17 exact
ranges: five of six three-frame swaps, six of six four-frame swaps, and
six of six four-frame swaps after recompression. The candidate found no
unrelated range in those edited files.

Only five of the 24 ordinary controls supplied enough measured context for
the new comparison. None of those five triggered an added range. The
remaining 19 controls do not establish the false-alarm behavior. The
ordinary coverage requirement was set before inference and the clock
extension remains experimental. A follow-up comparison targets ordinary
short movement and cuts that supply comparable face evidence.

Four physical remuxes with identical coded pictures reproduced the exact
decisions and crops on the alternate time base. Four invalid or unsupported
clock variants stayed outside the check. Independent decoding and recounting
verified 54 native anchors across 18 localized physical deliveries,
including the localized remux. These checks establish the retained picture
coordinates for the measured clips.
