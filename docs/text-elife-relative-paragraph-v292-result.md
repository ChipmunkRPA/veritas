# Relative paragraph comparison on new science explainers

On confirmation articles, the relative paragraph rule found **two of ten** known generated paragraphs where the existing check found **zero**. It added three false highlights across 13 wholly human articles and highlighted the wrong paragraph in two mixed articles. Both useful additions came from one of the two generators. [Frozen method](text-elife-relative-paragraph-v292-method.md) · [Exact aggregate](evaluation-panels/text-elife-relative-paragraph-v292-complete.json)

| Confirmation outcome | Existing check | With relative paragraph rule |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Useful location among eligible mixed articles | 0/10 | 2/10 |
| Useful location by generator | 0/5 and 0/5 | 0/5 and 2/5 |
| Entirely human article flagged | 0/13 | 3/13 |
| Mixed article with a highlight that misses the edit | 0/10 | 2/10 |
| Unchanged characters highlighted in mixed articles | 0/18,956 | 726/18,956 |

All 13 development originals had full coverage. Their responses fixed the point before any confirmation response was produced. The six guarded local batches recorded 331 classifier forwards, and the aggregate replay matched byte for byte. All 23 eligible confirmation articles had full coverage. Sixteen planned rewrites were unavailable and remain in the aggregate denominator for preparation.

The candidate met the coverage requirement. It fell short of the predefined useful-location criterion and exceeded the allowance for false highlights on human articles. The two recovered locations show where relative ranking may help as a corroborating cue. This experiment leaves the customer rule unchanged.
