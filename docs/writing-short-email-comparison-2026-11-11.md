# Sentence and body checks on short workplace email

The earlier workplace panel did not test AI rewrites in short messages. This comparison uses four new source groups of authentic work correspondence by U.S. federal employees. The original excerpts contain 35 to 56 words in one or two paragraphs. The topics include workshop coordination, a draft for internal review, a transmittal, and arranging a call. Only identifiable government-duty prose entered the comparison. One genuine human sentence replacement per original uses a second body by the same writer, except for one from another documented federal employee in the same office thread. These are controlled edits made from real source material. The excerpt and donor text shared no normalized 12-token fragment with another group or 45,247 prior local text entries. Two groups came from one archive packet. Authorship is disjoint, but institutional subject matter and unknown model training exposure limit independence. [Department of Energy release](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2021-05/HQ-2020-00688-F%20Final%20letter%20and%20Responsive%20documents.pdf) · [National Archives 2016 release](https://www.archives.gov/files/foia/NGC17-015%20Responsive%20Docs.pdf) · [National Archives State correspondence](https://www.archives.gov/foia/state-department-emails) · [U.S. government works](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html)

Each of two Apache-2.0 generators attempted to replace one sentence per source. The frozen checks required a terminal response, a single complete sentence of suitable length, and limited verbatim copying. Two of four Qwen attempts and three of four SmolLM attempts passed. One attempt each failed sentence structure, direct copying, or length. The five admitted rewrites preserved the exact text before and after the edit. The synthetic multipart EML fixtures preserved each selected body’s source positions and excluded the alternate HTML and a fixture attachment. The fixtures use neutral headers and do not test mail authentication. [Qwen revision and license](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct/blob/989aa7980e4cf806f80c7fef2b1adb7bc71aa306/LICENSE) · [SmolLM revision and license](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct/tree/31b70e2e869a7173562077fd711b654946d38674)

The baseline replays the current local classifier’s three-sentence evidence. The comparison uses an existing Apache-2.0 binary classifier with its publisher’s fixed decision point and temperature. It was previously evaluated on longer documents. Here it read each exact selected body and, in separate inputs, each source sentence of at least 12 words. The sentence check may give a more precise location because its input is the sentence it marks. The whole-body response can only support a body-level flag. Corroboration requires the sentence and body to both flag with that candidate. It uses one model at two scopes. Useful localization requires at least 0.30 non-whitespace character intersection over union with the known edit. [Candidate model and license](https://huggingface.co/rasbt/ai-text-detector-modernbert) · [Earlier checkpoint comparison](text-upgrade-evaluation.md)

## Results

All 13 admitted bodies passed exact MIME and source-map binding. The two classifiers read 44 current context windows, 13 candidate body inputs and 25 candidate sentence inputs. All five known edit sentences were eligible and every candidate input fit one complete token window. The two main scoring arms completed 30 CPU model forwards. A separate replay of the current email headline calibration completed seven further forwards. [Aggregate results and commitments](evaluation-panels/writing-short-email-comparison-2026-11-11.json)

The sentence-only check marked the wrong sentence in one AI-edited message and flagged three of eight human cases. Taking the union with the current passage result gave the same outcomes. Requiring corroboration identified one exact sentence with no human flags in this panel. The app’s existing calibrated email headline assessed four human cases and three mixed cases within its existing scope, with one AI flag and no human flags. Its one flagged body was the same case that corroboration usefully localized. The gain in that case was the location. Four human cases and two mixed cases fell outside the app’s headline reference scope.

The panel is small and uses one curated genre, two local generators, and short replacements that passed a fixed geometry gate. No operating point was fitted on these cases. This is a research result. The production evidence rules and confidence calculation remain as deployed.
