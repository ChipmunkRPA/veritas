# Separating ordinary image processing from localized inpainting

This development probe tests whether explicitly teaching a localizer the difference between an untouched photo and its autoencoder-only rendition helps detect edited pixels. The July DinoLizer revision motivates a third label for reconstruction. The experiment uses an independently implemented linear head on the pinned, Apache-2.0 general image-feature checkpoint already held locally. It uses no published inpainting detector, adapters, or training implementation. [Three-class inpainting study](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20722v2) · [Pinned checkpoint and license](https://huggingface.co/facebook/dinov2-small/tree/ed25f3a31f01632728cabb09d1542f84ab7b0056) · [Background restoration and matched controls](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00192v2)

## Matched training and controls

For each source, use the exact original, publisher-generated edit and publisher-matched autoencoder pass. Compose the autoencoder view into the exact same changed-pixel footprint as the inpainted edit, preserving original source pixels outside it. That makes a local blend or affected footprint insufficient by itself to distinguish the two edited classes. Both classifier arms see the same patch coordinates and exact JPEG75 deliveries. The binary arm groups the original and autoencoder-only examples as negative. The three-class arm fits those labels separately and scores the inpainted logit relative to both negative logits. Both use the same fixed ridge strength and training groups. Every admitted group contributes 12 matched coordinate triplets. Exclude one Kandinsky source with zero qualifying coordinates and one SDXL source with six under the fixed site rule. The fit uses 18 photo groups and 648 individual token observations. Source groups, rather than tokens, are the relevant independent units.

The fit groups cover five SDXL, five Kandinsky and eight Stable Diffusion 1.5 photos. Eight separate Stable Diffusion 1.5 groups supply only ordinary calibration controls, including the original, exact-site and full-image autoencoder views, blur and same-photo copy. Twenty held groups include six SDXL, six Kandinsky and eight Stable Diffusion 1.5. Test variants include sharp and feathered edits, those five ordinary views, and local tone controls. All photos had earlier project exposure under other methods. The private manifest verified distinct recipients, photo IDs, credited owners, exact bytes, decoded RGB and informative thumbnails across roles. Individual photos are CC BY 2.0, publisher contributions are CC BY 4.0, and the generator records include their underlying usage terms. LaMa lacks the same exact processed-class pairing and is outside this development probe. ## Native source localization

Process nonoverlapping 224-pixel tiles, preserving their positions and reflecting only the necessary right and bottom borders. Read two intermediate layers on a shared native 14-pixel token grid. Unit-normalize the two layer vectors separately and concatenate. Crop any incomplete source-border cells from the evidence grid. The highest compact rectangle must beat its surrounding ring and the image median. Both heads use identical rectangle scoring and exact overlap requirements. Compare against the current aligned JPEG luma check on the same bytes. A useful region needs intersection over union of at least 0.30 and recovery of at least 0.50 of the exact changed pixels. Set each arm's point from the second-highest ordinary calibration group maximum, before scoring held images. The held criterion calls for at least three additional useful feathered groups across two families, at most two added ordinary groups and at most four misplaced edited-file flags. The probe remains outside runtime.

## Held results

| Check | Useful sharp regions | Useful feathered regions | Ordinary files flagged | Ordinary source groups flagged | Misplaced edited-file flags |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Current JPEG luma | 0/20 | 0/20 | 0/120 | 0/20 | 0/40 |
| Binary head | 3/20 | 3/20 | 19/120 | 5/20 | 6/40 |
| Three-class head | 3/20 | 3/20 | 17/120 | 3/20 | 6/40 |

Both learned checks located the same three feathered regions missed by JPEG luma, all from Stable Diffusion 1.5. Each recovered 3/8 feathered and 3/8 sharp cases from that family. Before imposing the score point, the best rectangles already overlapped useful regions in only four feathered cases for the binary arm and three for the three-class arm. The leading boxes often missed the edit itself. The extra processing class cut ordinary flags from 19 to 17 files and from five to three source groups. It left the same six misplaced flags and fell short of the breadth and ordinary-control criteria. The 120 ordinary files include six correlated renditions from each photo group.

The offline run took 101.53 seconds with one CPU thread. It read 254 JPEG deliveries in 3,156 native tiles across 789 batches. A separate readback reproduced 200 calibration and held JPEG and mask bindings, 560 box-overlap calculations, 480 held flag decisions and the 54 JPEG deliveries and selected coordinates used in fitting. Readback uses the saved coordinates and scores. The model run is reproducible from the pinned checkpoint and retained source inputs.
