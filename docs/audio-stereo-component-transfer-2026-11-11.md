# Stereo component separation for speech screening

The preceding frequency-coherence trial recovered nine synthetic flags and added three false flags on genuine readers. This comparison tests a different way to use two microphones. It estimates two whole-window components from the observed channels and checks whether a second view changes the fixed voice decision. [Preceding coherence trial](audio-natural-stereo-coherence-2026-11-11.md) · [Exact aggregate](evaluation-panels/audio-stereo-component-transfer-2026-11-11.json)

## Fixed inputs and method

The panel uses two newly acquired AMI meeting sessions. The publisher supplies 16 kHz aligned microphone WAVs and licenses the corpus CC BY 4.0. Both sessions are distinct from the earlier AMI stereo panels. Take the first six three-second windows per session on the `45 + 30 × i` second grid with mono RMS at least 0.002. Replay all 12 untouched controls. The window with the largest channel-difference-to-average RMS ratio in each session is the interfering recording for that scene. It can contain human speech. Exact source hashes and clocks are bound in the local receipt. [AMI license](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/corpus/license.shtml) · [ES2002a recording index](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/AMICorpusMirror/amicorpus/ES2002a/audio/) · [IS1003a recording index](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/AMICorpusMirror/amicorpus/IS1003a/audio/) · [AMI capture notes](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/corpus/signals.shtml)

Eight genuine foregrounds come from distinct LibriSpeech and VoxForge readers, five English and three Turkish. The synthetic foregrounds contain two Alania designed voices, two Okur utterances from one stock voice and four Drifting utterances from three stock voices. All 16 exact clips were unused in the two immediately preceding temporal and stereo score runs. Several generator and recording banks have older exposure. The public result counts older Drifting reuse separately. General model pretraining overlap is unknown. [Previous temporal source and rights review](audio-temporal-corroboration-2026-11-11.md) · [Drifting source review](audio-drifting-turkish-v215-method.md)

Each foreground contributes a gain-matched clean input and two stereo mixtures at −5 dB foreground-to-background mono SNR. The foreground enters both channels equally. Each background retains the relative levels and timing from the original microphones. There are 48 controlled views plus 12 unchanged native views. This geometry tests a centered foreground in recorded interference. Foregrounds at other positions and audio with one channel remain outside this comparison.

Whiten the two zero-mean microphone channels by their whole-window covariance. Search 90 fixed orientations in a right angle. Choose the orientation with the largest sum of absolute excess kurtoses of the two orthogonal projected signals. Use the component whose native mixing vector is closer to the equal-channel direction. Require absolute cosine alignment of at least 0.90, a margin of at least 0.05, and an eigenvalue ratio of at least 0.0001. Scale its two projection weights to sum to one, cap their absolute values at 1.5 and apply them to the original uncentered sample pairs. The other component uses weights whose absolute values sum to one. No clipping, spectral gain mask, or padding is applied. This is a two-channel independent-component projection under a centered target assumption. The two outputs share source samples and model calibration. [Independent-component analysis method](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/ahyvarin/papers/NC97.pdf)

The original mono and each available projection pass through the unchanged speech gate. Classifier operating points are fixed. The primary policy promotes a current inconclusive result only if the center projection leans AI and the other projection is assessed without an AI direction. A second comparison checks the same center-only promotion without the other component. Both preserve all existing determinate and unassessed results. Advancement requires at least two distinct synthetic voice groups recovered with at least one recovery per scene, at most one new genuine flag, and at least six original mono admissions among genuine readers in each scene. Separate reporting of speech and interference follows the component-level evaluation question identified by recent research. [MADBench component-level evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09593) · [AT-ADD channel and generator transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23437v2)

Both candidate policies returned the same directions as current mono on all 60 views. They recovered zero additional synthetic flags and added zero genuine flags. The frozen advancement criterion failed.

The center component was available for all 32 noisy mixtures and 11 of 12 native controls. All 16 identical-channel clean inputs had rank-one covariance and therefore supplied no independent component. The center projection and mono had the same noisy speech-admission counts. The other projection admitted zero inputs in the first scene and all 16 in the second. It leaned AI on two mixtures with a synthetic foreground in the second scene. Its signal remains a derived mixture, so these are ambiguous auxiliary responses about the scene. Six of the eight distinct genuine readers had a false AI direction under at least one recorded background, and two had it under both.

Known separate components reproduce the original mixture and the candidate's linear projection. The median center-projection SNR change was +0.005 dB in the first scene and −0.133 dB in the second. The little measured acoustic gain agrees with the absence of a classification gain here. Four reused Drifting utterances were present in the earlier kitchen and traffic study. The 16 controlled foregrounds repeat across the two rooms, and the 12 native windows share two meeting sessions. These are paired results within selected sources.

The existing inference remains in service at the same operating points. This comparison shows severe genuine-speech false alarms in new rooms even when the speech gate admits the windows. Strong interference or overlapping speech therefore remains an unresolved reliability issue. It is distinct from the app's exact unscored voice coverage.
