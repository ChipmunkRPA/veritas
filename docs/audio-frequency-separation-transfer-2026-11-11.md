# Frequency-domain separation for competing speech

This local comparison tests whether separating the same stereo input before scoring can recover synthetic speech obscured by competing voices. Earlier broadband separation recovered no additional synthetic flags. A frequency-bin coherence filter recovered some synthetic flags and added genuine false flags. The new comparison freezes an independent-vector separation before scoring and requires its acoustic and screening effects to be measured separately. ## Frozen comparison

Use each exact three-second stereo input at 16 kHz. Compute a 512-sample periodic Hann transform with a 128-sample hop. Estimate two complex source components using 18 auxiliary-function iterative-projection updates and a shared per-frame radius from 250 Hz to 5 kHz. A fixed diagonal regularizer stabilizes weighted covariance. Back-project both components to the average of the original microphone channels. Choose the component with greater source-image-energy-weighted alignment to the center across that frequency band. A component qualifies at alignment of at least 0.80 and an advantage of at least 0.03. Reject weak stereo rank, unresolved center selection, and invalid output. The NumPy implementation uses the published algorithmic method and no additional model or checkpoint. [Ono, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1109/ASPAA.2011.6082320) · [Brendel and Kellermann, 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.09402) · [Implementation](../backend/scripts/audio_frequency_separation.py)

Compare the FFmpeg production mono downmix with that one projection on the same exact sample clock. Both use the same speech gate, classifier and calibration. The combination may change a current inconclusive result to AI only if the projection passes the speech gate and independently reaches the existing AI direction. Current AI, original and unassessed directions are preserved. Advancement requires at least two synthetic voice groups recovered across the two scenes with at least one recovery in each, at most one additional false AI flag across all views, and at least six baseline-admitted genuine inputs per noisy scene. A pass warrants a larger independent panel.

For the acoustic development check, replay known centered foregrounds in two previously consumed AMI rooms. Do not use voice classifier results in this role. The 56 confirmation views use two separate AMI meetings with time cuts disjoint from earlier scored project cuts. Each of eight genuine RUEG readers and eight synthetic utterances from Alania, Okur and Drifting is assessed clean and in two matched −5 dB mono mixtures. Four untouched natural stereo windows per room supply additional genuine controls. The mixing foreground is centered in both microphones, while the interfering speech keeps its recorded stereo image. The confirmation source banks and rooms had older project exposure. Exact foreground groups and the meeting sessions are disjoint from this experiment's acoustic development role. The predeclared room selector uses a fixed grid and manual AMI word times, independent of authenticity scores. Both matched backgrounds contain 3.00 seconds with at least one annotated speaker. The first contains 0.09 seconds of simultaneous annotated words and the second contains 1.76 seconds. The first selected window from each room also appears among the untouched controls. Each scene and foreground counts as one matched source relationship even though its waveform is evaluated in more than one view.

The combined rule recovered one Drifting voice in the overlapping scene without adding genuine false flags. The remaining synthetic clips without an AI flag were inconclusive, including three in each noisy scene under the combined rule. Scoring the projection alone would also have lost one correct flag in the mostly-single-interferer scene. Genuine assessment was limited, and the single-interferer scene fell below the frozen six-input genuine coverage requirement. The current directions and customer confidence remain unchanged.

The known-source audit applied the same coefficients fitted on the mixture to its separately retained clean foreground and background. Their recombined projection matched the actual projected waveform with maximum error below 0.00000006 in normalized PCM. All 32 noisy mixtures gained SNR in this audit. Median gains were 1.54 dB and 3.99 dB, with median foreground level changes of −0.24 dB and −0.35 dB, respectively. Both consumed development scenes also gained SNR in all 20 examined mixtures. Those measured gains do not establish the origin of unknown audio. The design favors a known centered foreground and evaluates just two confirmation recordings. It does not establish how reliably the same selection can isolate a speaker in arbitrary stereo or mono uploads.

The run completed 8,930 speech-gate frame forwards and 96 classifier forwards. The classifier assessed 50 original mono and 29 projected inputs, with 17 additional stability forwards. It took 61.30 seconds on CPU and reached 2.93 GB process RSS. A repeat reproduced the frozen source roster, all 39 available projected PCM digests and acoustic measurements. An independent readout reconstructed each observed decision from the frozen calibration and stability result, verified both speech-gate bindings, matched the actual forward count, and reproduced the aggregate. Exact speech samples, per-case scores and projection coefficients remain local.
